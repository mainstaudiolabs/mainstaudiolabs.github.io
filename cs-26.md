## Welcome

**CS-26** is a compressor for guitar that adds sustain, and the first pedal in the line that is entirely our own design. We drew the circuit from scratch, built it on paper part by part and simulated it the same way we simulate the classic pedals and amps: every part of the circuit is solved in real time, not sampled.

It was designed to be **transparent**. Your guitar, your pickups and your amp still sound like themselves; what changes is the dynamics. Soft notes come up, hard picking gets tamed, and long notes ring longer.

- **SUSTAIN** sets how much it compresses.
- **ATTACK** decides how much of the pick attack gets through before the compressor grabs the note.
- **BLEND** mixes your clean signal back in, so the attack stays alive even with a lot of compression.
- **RATIO** goes from gentle to limiting, and **BASS** keeps low notes from pumping the compressor.
- A row of **LEDs** shows how hard it is working.

**Free, forever.** No license key, no time limit and nothing locked. If the CS-26 earns a place on your board, the [tip jar](https://payhip.com/b/7XO0F) keeps the lights on.

## Where to Put It

### 1. First on the guitar track (the default)

A compressor usually goes first, right after the guitar: before your drive pedals and before your amp simulator. Insert **CS-26** at the top of the guitar track. The input is calibrated like our amps, to the voltage of a real guitar, so SUSTAIN behaves like it would on a pedal at the front of your board.

### 2. After a drive pedal or the amp

After a drive it tames the level jumps and smooths the decay; after the amp it works more like a studio compressor on the track. Mix-level signals are much hotter than a guitar, so lower **INPUT** there, or the compressor will work harder than you want.

## Controls

- **SUSTAIN (0–10):** How much it compresses. Low settings only catch the hardest notes; turn it up and even soft playing is lifted and held, and long notes ring longer. The factory setting (0.5) is a gentle touch that evens out your picking without being heard.
- **ATTACK (0–10):** How quickly it grabs each note. At 0 (the default) it catches the pick right away, for an even, smooth sound. Turn it up and more of the pick attack gets through before the compressor clamps down, so the playing sounds punchier.
- **BLEND (0–10):** The mix between your clean signal (0) and the compressed one (10). In between, the clean signal brings back the attack and the natural dynamics while the compressed one adds the sustain underneath. The default is 9.2.
- **VOLUME (0–10):** The output level. At **6.3** the pedal is at the same level as bypass, with any SUSTAIN setting; from there up to +12 dB of boost. The compressor never pushes the level up by itself: VOLUME is the only control that does.
- **RATIO (2:1 / 4:1 / LIM):** How hard it compresses past the threshold. **2:1** is gentle, **4:1** (the default) is firm and **LIM** works as a limiter. The knee is soft in all three, so the compressor eases in rather than kicking in.
- **BASS (FLAT / 100 Hz):** At **100 Hz** the compressor ignores most of the lows when it decides how much to compress, so the low strings and palm mutes stop pumping it. The sound itself is not filtered: only what the compressor listens to.
- **COMPRESSION LEDs:** Six LEDs that light up at **3, 6, 9, 12, 15 and 20 dB** of gain reduction.
- **The footswitch:** Turns the effect on and off. The switch crossfades in a few milliseconds, so there is no click and no gap. The **EFFECT** LED shows when it's on.
- **POWER ON:** Turns the whole plugin off. The signal passes untouched and the plugin uses **0% CPU**. It's different from the footswitch: the footswitch is the pedal's bypass, POWER switches the plugin off.

Double-click a knob to return it to its default.

## The Bottom Panel

- **INPUT (−24 to +24 dB):** How hard the signal hits the pedal.
- **OUT TRIM (−36 to +24 dB):** The final level, after VOLUME.
- **GAIN REDUCTION:** A 12-segment meter with the reading in dB: how much the compressor is turning the level down right now.
- **INFO:** What the controls do, the version, the website and the tip jar.
- **OVERSAMPLING (1×, 2×, 4×):** **2×** is the default. 1× uses half the CPU; 4× is there if you want it. 2× and 4× add 3 and 4 samples of latency, reported to your DAW. Oversampling counts from 48 kHz: if your project runs at 88.2 or 96 kHz, the pedal already works at that rate, so 2× costs nothing extra there.

You can resize the window from its corner; it keeps its size with your project.

**Silence costs nothing.** After a moment of digital silence on the track (gaps between regions, a stopped transport) the pedal rests and the CPU drops to almost zero. The first note wakes it up instantly, right where it left off.

## Setting INPUT

The pedal responds to the **actual voltage** at its input jack, like our amps. `0 dBFS` corresponds to **4.62 V peak** at the jack (a Focusrite Scarlett 3rd gen at minimum gain, INST input). With an interface like that, leave INPUT at 0 dB. If your interface records the guitar hotter or quieter, use INPUT to bring it to that level. With the factory settings the compressor only touches the hardest notes; turn SUSTAIN up and watch the LEDs to hear it work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why doesn't the level go up when I turn up SUSTAIN?**  
On purpose. Many compressors raise the level automatically as they compress, and on a guitar that turns into a boost on every pick attack. CS-26 doesn't: VOLUME at 6.3 is the bypass level, and only VOLUME adds level.

**Where are the release and the threshold?**  
SUSTAIN sets the threshold. The release follows your playing on its own: fast after a short, hard note and slower after a long one, so the sustain doesn't swell or pump.

**Why is it mono?**  
Like a pedal, it is mono. A stereo input is summed to mono, and both outputs carry the same signal.

**Is it really free?**  
Yes. No key, no time limit, nothing locked. The tip jar is there if you want to support the lab.
