## Welcome

**TS '82** is the green overdrive of 1982, the Ibanez TS9 Tube Screamer, rebuilt part by part. It is not a sample or a profile: the whole circuit is solved in real time, with the same parts doing the same work. It's the TS9 as it was, with no TS808 switch and no mods.

- **The buffers:** two Toshiba 2SC1815 transistors, at the input and the output, as in the pedal.
- **The clipping stage:** a **JRC4558D** op-amp with a pair of silicon diodes in its feedback loop. A 720 Hz high-pass in that loop clips the mids and leaves the lows clean: that is the famous mid hump and the tight low end.
- **The tone stage:** the active TONE control with the original pot.
- **The bypass:** the original buffered bypass, with its JFET switching.

That's why it smooths out your amp instead of fighting it: on its own it's a warm, mid-forward overdrive, and in front of a dirty amp it tightens the lows and makes the amp sing.

**Free, forever.** No license key, no time limit and nothing locked. If TS '82 earns a place on your board, the [tip jar](https://payhip.com/b/7XO0F) keeps the lights on.

## Where to Put It

### 1. In front of your amp (the default)

Put **TS '82** first on the guitar track, before your amp simulator (or before the amp you re-amp through). The input is calibrated like our amps, to the voltage of a real guitar, so the clipping reacts to your picking like the pedal.

- **Overdrive on its own:** DRIVE from the middle up, LEVEL to taste, with a clean or slightly crunchy amp.
- **Boost into a dirty amp:** DRIVE low, LEVEL up. The classic use: tighter lows, more mids, a singing amp.

### 2. Stacked with another drive

In front of another overdrive or a fuzz, DRIVE low and LEVEL up tightens and focuses it.

## Controls

- **DRIVE (0–10):** How much it overdrives. The pot has the audio taper of the original, so most of the change happens in the first half.
- **TONE (0–10):** Darker to brighter. The original pot taper, too: the middle of the dial is where most players live.
- **LEVEL (0–10):** The output level.
- **The footswitch:** Turns the effect on and off. Off, the signal goes through the pedal's original buffered bypass (the transistor buffers stay in the path, as in the real one). The ring around it lights up when it's on.
- **POWER ON:** Turns the whole plugin off. The signal passes untouched and the plugin uses **0% CPU**. It's different from the footswitch: the footswitch is the pedal's bypass, POWER switches the plugin off.

Double-click a knob to return it to its default.

---

## The Bottom Panel

- **INPUT (−24 to +24 dB):** How hard the signal hits the pedal. Below its meter, the peak voltage reaching the pedal (V pk).
- **OUTPUT (−36 to +24 dB):** The final level. Below its meter, the output level in dBFS.
- **INFO:** What the controls do, the version, the website and the tip jar.
- **OVERSAMPLING (1×, 2×, 4×):** Reduces aliasing when the pedal clips. **4×** is the default, because high gain on high notes needs it; 1× and 2× use a quarter and half of the CPU. 2× and 4× add 3 and 4 samples of latency, reported to your DAW. Oversampling counts from 48 kHz: at 96 kHz each setting runs one step lower and costs the same.

You can resize the window from its corner; it keeps its size with your project.

**Silence costs nothing.** After a moment of digital silence on the track (gaps between regions, a stopped transport) the pedal rests and the CPU drops to almost zero. The first note wakes it up instantly, right where it left off.

## Setting INPUT

The pedal responds to the **actual voltage** at its input jack, like our amps. `0 dBFS` corresponds to **4.62 V peak** at the jack (a Focusrite Scarlett 3rd gen at minimum gain, INST input). Strum hard and adjust INPUT until the reading under the input meter lands in the range of your pickups:

| Pickup type | Normal playing | Hard strum |
|---|---|---|
| **Vintage single coil** (Strat, Tele) | 0.05 – 0.10 V | **0.3 – 0.5 V** |
| **Hot single coil, P-90** | 0.10 – 0.20 V | **0.5 – 0.8 V** |
| **Humbucker** (PAF style) | 0.10 – 0.25 V | **0.6 – 1.0 V** |
| **Hot humbucker** | 0.20 – 0.40 V | **1.0 – 1.5 V** |
| **Active pickups** | 0.30 – 0.60 V | **1.5 – 2.5 V** |

That matters for an overdrive: the clipping depends on how hard you hit it, just like the real pedal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it a TS808?**  
No: it's the TS9 of 1982, as it was. The two are close; the TS9's output stage is a little brighter.

**Why does it have so much mids and so little bass?**  
That's the circuit: a high-pass at 720 Hz inside the clipping stage leaves the lows clean and pushes the mids. It's what makes it cut through a mix and tighten a dirty amp.

**Why is it mono?**  
Like a pedal, it is mono. A stereo input is summed to mono, and both outputs carry the same signal.

**Is it really free?**  
Yes. No key, no time limit, nothing locked. The tip jar is there if you want to support the lab.
